Deep dive

The five study rooms, opened up

AI Talk, the Reader, the PDF tool, Vocabulary and Sentences, each tried hands-on. What's inside, where it falls short, and the Funlingo angle.

The biggest gap: Trancy's translations miss idioms

Sentence packs and subtitles use Google Translate by default. In the "Everyday English" pack, with Hindi as the native language, several common phrases came out wrong:

  • "Same old, same old." Translated word for word into a phrase that means nothing in Hindi.
  • "How have you been?" Cut off to a fragment, roughly "Are you?"
  • "I've been better, honestly." Rendered as "I have gotten better", the opposite of what it means.
  • "Hey, how's it going?" Translated literally, where a Hindi speaker would just say "और, क्या हाल है?"

Trancy's paid AI Sentence Analysis fixes this one line at a time. Funlingo's "translate the idiom, never the words" rule can make this the default for every line, for free.

AI Talk

Paid
Structure
About 110 topics across Work, Travel, Social, Shopping and School. Each opens into 2–11 lessons; Small Talk has Weather, Weekend Plans, Movies & TV, Food, Music, Current Events, Elevator Talk and more.
A lesson
A one-paragraph scene, two roles you can swap, AI difficulty (Beginner / Intermediate / Advanced), four optional tasks, 25 shadowing sentences, then the AI roleplay.
The chat
The AI opens. Every line shows a translation with replay, hide-text and save buttons. You answer by mic or text. "AI Assistant" writes a full suggested reply you can listen to or send. Settings choose the AI voice, your voice, speed, auto-translation and a hide-text listening mode.
Gap
I replied "Yes, I am enjoy the sun very much today". The AI simply carried on: no correction, no hint, no end-of-chat report. Feedback is only pronunciation scoring, and that's behind Premium.

Funlingo angle: gentle inline fixes ("you can say: I'm enjoying the sun"), a short recap of mistakes after each chat, and scenes drawn from the shows users just watched.

Free

What to build first

Summary

Of these five, two are worth copying almost as-is: the Reader and the Sentences practice player. Two are worth copying and improving: AI Talk (add corrections) and flashcards (use real scenes). The PDF tool can wait. Across all of them, natural idiom-aware translation is the one change Trancy's users would notice first.

Is it legal?

Mostly yes. One shelf, no.

The Movie shelf is the one to stay away from

Film dialogue and its translated subtitles are copyrighted, separately from the video. Trancy stores the whole subtitle track of commercial films, including song lyrics, and serves it as study material. Leaving out the picture doesn't change that. Without a licence from the studio or subtitle owner, this is reproduction of a copyrighted work. It's the kind of feature that draws takedown notices, and for a small team, an app-store complaint is enough to hurt.

YouTube player

Embedding is allowed. The risk sits around the edges: YouTube's API rules ban scraping and storing captions without permission, and "Export subtitles" hands users a copy of someone else's transcript. Charging can't gate playback. Safer pattern: fetch captions only in the user's browser through the extension, keep nothing server-side, and drop the export button. Two more to skip: "AI transcribe" re-transcribes the YouTube audio on Trancy's servers (YouTube forbids downloading or separating audio), and Theater layout draws subtitles over the YouTube player.

Word lists

Single words aren't protected, but a curated list can be (Oxford 5000 is Oxford's product; the COCA list is sold under licence). Build Funlingo lists from frequency data you're allowed to use, or from words your users actually save.

Books

Public-domain classics are free to use. The children's readers are Creative Commons; most require credit to the author, illustrator and publisher on every book. Easy to do right.

AI Talk, PDFs, your own saves

Original prompts, the user's own files and the user's own words. No third-party content involved.

This is a product-level read, not legal advice. Get a lawyer to check the caption setup before launch, especially for users in India, the US and the EU, where the rules differ.

Replace the Movie shelf with "Show Packs"

For each popular show, link straight to it on the user's own streaming account, where the extension handles subtitles live. Add a Funlingo-written pack of the slang, idioms and cultural references the show uses, in your own words. Learners get the same "study this film" pull without you hosting a single line of the script.

One deck from everywhere

Trancy's hub is mostly fed by YouTube. Funlingo's extension already sees Netflix, Prime Video, Hotstar and Crunchyroll. Every word saved there should land in the hub with the scene it came from. That's the feature Trancy can't easily match.

Culture notes, not summaries

Trancy summarises what a video says. Funlingo should explain why a line is funny, rude or regional. This is the Cultural Context Engine, and it's your own content, so it's legally clean too.

AI Talk with local scenes

Trancy's role-plays are generic (cruise travel, warehouse clubs). Funlingo can do scenes your users actually live: a Mumbai local, a chai stall, a family WhatsApp call, an anime convention.
