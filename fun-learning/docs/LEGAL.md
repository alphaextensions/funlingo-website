# Content rules (hard rules)

These come from the Trancy teardown and YouTube's API Services Developer Policies. They are product rules, not legal advice; get a lawyer to review the caption and podcast setup before launch.

## Never build
1. **Movie or TV scripts / subtitle tracks** of commercial films or series, in any form (full text, sentence lists, TTS read-outs, "practice packs"). Trancy's Movie shelf does this; we don't. Use Show Packs (our own writing) instead.
2. **Song lyrics** from any copyrighted song.
3. **Subtitle export / download** of YouTube or podcast transcripts.
4. **Episode download** buttons for podcasts.
5. **Server-side extraction of YouTube audio or video** (including "AI re-transcribe" of a YouTube video). YouTube policy forbids downloading, separating or modifying audio/video.
6. **Anything drawn over the YouTube iframe**, or anything that hides YouTube's branding, links or ads. Subtitles go beside or below the player.
7. **Charging for, or gating, YouTube playback.** Paid features (if ever) must be our own features.

## Rules for third-party content
- **YouTube captions** are fetched in the learner's browser only. They are never sent to, stored in or cached by our servers. Our servers may receive single lines transiently to translate or explain them, and must not log or persist them.
- **YouTube metadata** (title, channel, thumbnail, duration) may be stored, refreshed or deleted within 30 days per YouTube policy. Show "YouTube" as the source with YouTube branding on pages that show YouTube content.
- **Podcasts**: stream audio from the publisher's enclosure URL; use publisher transcripts when provided; speech-to-text for others needs a legal decision first. Always show the show name and link to the publisher.
- **User's saved lines**: a single line the user saves as a personal note is stored privately for that user only, never shown to others, deleted with their account.
- **Word lists**: don't copy proprietary lists (Oxford 5000, commercial exam lists, licensed COCA data). Build our own from openly licensed data.

## Openly licensed books
- Public-domain classics (Standard Ebooks, Project Gutenberg, Aozora Bunko): fine; Gutenberg's trademark rules mean we drop their header/branding if we redistribute.
- Creative Commons readers (StoryWeaver, African Storybook, Book Dash, Global Digital Library, Let's Read): show author, illustrator, translator, publisher, licence name and link at the start and end of every book. Respect NC/ND terms if any book carries them (exclude ND books from any feature that modifies text, e.g. simplification).

## Content safety for generated text
Generated examples, culture notes and Talk replies must not link groups to crime or terrorism, use slurs or stereotypes, or sexual content. Run outputs through a content filter and keep a "report" button on every card.
